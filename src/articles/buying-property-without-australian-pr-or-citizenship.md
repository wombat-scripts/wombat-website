Last checked: 18 November 2026

**The short answer:** yes, many visa holders can buy a home in Australia. What you can buy, and how much a lender will lend, comes down to your visa. Permanent residents living here are treated much like citizens. Temporary residents need FIRB approval, can't buy an established home until 30 June 2029, and in NSW usually pay a 9% surcharge on top of normal stamp duty. Partner visa holders buying with a citizen or PR spouse often have the easiest path.

Everyone's situation is different, so this is general information, not advice.

## Are you a "foreign person" for property rules?

For federal property rules you're generally a foreign person if you're not an Australian citizen, not a permanent resident who's ordinarily resident here, and not a New Zealand citizen on a special category visa (subclass 444). Temporary visa holders, including most bridging visa holders, are foreign persons.

State tax rules use their own tests. In NSW, holding PR isn't enough on its own. More on that below.

## How does your visa type change what a lender will do?

Lenders set their own rules, and they change. As a rough guide:

| Your situation | FIRB approval? | What you can buy | Typical lending (varies by lender) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Permanent resident living in Australia | No | Any home, including established | Close to a citizen. Many lenders go up to 95% of the price with lenders mortgage insurance |
| Partner visa (309/820), buying as joint tenants with a citizen or PR spouse | No | Any home, including established | Often assessed like a normal couple. Lender limits range from about 70% to 95% |
| Partner visa, buying alone | Yes | New homes, off-the-plan or land | Limited. Treated like a temporary resident |
| Skilled temporary visa (such as 482, 491, 494) | Yes | New homes, off-the-plan or land | A smaller group of lenders, usually 70% to 90%. Some want 12 months or more left on the visa |
| Student, graduate (485) or working holiday visa | Yes | New homes, off-the-plan or land | Mainstream lenders generally say no. Very few specialist options |
| NZ citizen on a 444 visa | No | Any home, including established | Often close to PR, but check the lender |

Lenders also look at time left on your visa, whether you're the main visa holder, and whether you're paid in Australian dollars.

## Do you need FIRB approval?

If you're a temporary resident, yes. You need approval before you buy, whatever the price. The application is lodged with the ATO, and the contract should be conditional on approval.

The fee is tiered by price and changes each year. For new homes or vacant land bought between 1 July 2026 and 30 June 2027, the ATO lists $15,600 for a property up to $1 million and $31,300 up to $2 million. Check the ATO's current table before you sign.

Permanent residents, citizens and NZ citizens on a 444 visa don't need FIRB approval.

## Can temporary residents buy an established home?

Not right now. From 1 April 2025, foreign persons, including temporary residents, are banned from buying established homes. The 2026-27 Budget extended the ban to 30 June 2029. Older articles still quote March 2027. That's out of date.

With approval, you can still buy:

- a new or near-new home
- an off-the-plan apartment or house
- vacant land to build on (it has to be built on within four years)

There are narrow exceptions for big redevelopments that add 20 or more homes, and for some employer-provided worker housing. They aren't a way for a household to buy an established home.

Knocking down one house and building one new one still counts as buying an established home.

## What extra costs do foreign buyers pay in NSW?

Two big ones, on top of normal costs:

- **Surcharge purchaser duty: 9%** of the price, on top of normal stamp duty. Revenue NSW's own example is a $1 million purchase attracting $90,000 in surcharge duty plus $39,187 in standard duty.
- **Surcharge land tax: 5%** of the land value every year, from the 2025 land tax year. There's no tax-free threshold, so it applies even when normal land tax doesn't.

If you buy with someone who isn't a foreign person, the surcharge is generally worked out on the foreign buyer's share only.

Foreign owners also have to lodge a vacancy fee return with the ATO each year, and can be charged a fee if the home is left empty.

## What is the 200-day test?

This catches a lot of PR holders out. In NSW, a permanent resident only avoids the 9% surcharge duty if they've been in Australia for at least 200 days in the 12 months before the contract date. If you don't meet that, there's a second path: live in the home you're buying, as your main residence, for at least 200 days in the 12 months after the contract.

Partner visa holders (309/820) can use the same tests for surcharge duty.

Land tax uses a different count. A PR needs 200 days in Australia in the calendar year. Spend 165 days or more overseas in a year and the 5% surcharge can apply.

If you paid the surcharge at exchange and you're no longer a foreign person by the date the property transfers to you, for example because PR came through, you may be able to apply to Revenue NSW for a refund. It isn't automatic.

## Can buying with your partner avoid FIRB?

Yes, if it's done properly. If you're a temporary resident and you buy as **joint tenants** with your spouse or de facto partner who's an Australian citizen, a permanent resident or an eligible NZ citizen, the purchase is exempt from FIRB. That means the established-home ban doesn't apply either.

The details matter:

- It has to be joint tenants. Tenants in common doesn't qualify.
- Your partner's citizenship or PR has to already be granted.
- Tell your conveyancer before the contract is drawn up.
- It doesn't switch off NSW surcharges. Your share can still be surcharged if you don't meet the 200-day tests.

Most lenders will want both of you on the loan.

## What about trusts, nominees or a relative buying for you?

Steer clear. You'll see these sold online as loopholes. They aren't.

If a citizen buys a home that's really for a foreign relative, FIRB looks through to the real owner. The ATO is targeting this, and the results can include forced sale orders and penalties. A company or trust with a foreign beneficiary is usually treated as a foreign person anyway, which brings FIRB and the NSW surcharges straight back in.

A family guarantor can help with a loan, but it doesn't change anyone's FIRB status.

## Can visa holders use first home buyer schemes?

Mostly no. The 5% Deposit Scheme is for citizens and permanent residents only, and every applicant on the loan has to qualify. Help to Buy is for citizens only. The NSW first home buyer duty concession needs at least one buyer to be a citizen or PR, and it doesn't waive the 9% surcharge.

## FAQ

**Can I buy a home on a temporary skilled visa?**
Yes, but with FIRB approval, and only a new home, off-the-plan or land until 30 June 2029. A smaller group of lenders will lend.

**Do permanent residents pay the NSW foreign buyer surcharge?**
Not if they meet the 200-day test, or live in the home for 200 days in the 12 months after the contract. PR on its own isn't enough.

**Can I buy an established home with my Australian partner?**
Yes, if you buy as joint tenants and your partner is a citizen, PR or eligible NZ citizen. No FIRB approval is needed.

**I'm on a student visa. Can I get a home loan?**
Realistically, very hard. Mainstream lenders generally don't lend to student, graduate or working holiday visa holders.
