Moving from a temporary visa to permanent residency? Here's how your property buying rights and foreign purchaser surcharges change in Australia, and the one test that catches a lot of new PR holders out.

Last checked: 18 November 2026

Gaining Australian permanent residency (PR) changes your position under the **Foreign Acquisitions and Takeovers Act 1975**, and can significantly reduce your property costs. Across Australia, PR means you no longer need federal (FIRB) approval to buy. State surcharges are a separate question, and in some states, such as NSW, PR on its own doesn't switch them off.

Everyone's situation is different, so this is general information, not advice.

## Before PR: You're a "Foreign Person"

If you hold a temporary visa, the ATO considers you a foreign person for residential property. This means:

- You must apply for approval through the ATO before you buy.
- You can only buy certain property types (e.g. new dwellings, off-the-plan, vacant land with build requirements).
- You can't buy an established home. Foreign persons, including temporary residents, are banned from buying established homes from 1 April 2025 to 30 June 2029, with limited exceptions.
- Foreign purchaser surcharges and land tax surcharges apply.

## After PR: No FIRB Approval, But Check the Surcharges

Once you hold a permanent resident visa, you don't need ATO (FIRB) approval to buy residential property, and the ban on established homes no longer applies to you.

The state surcharges are a different question. Each state and territory has its own test, and most treat permanent residents as not foreign. Two examples:

- In **NSW** (example), PR alone isn't enough. To avoid the 9% surcharge purchaser duty, you need to have been in Australia for at least 200 days in the 12 months before the contract date. If you don't meet that, you can still be exempt if you live in the home you're buying, as your main residence, for at least 200 days in the 12 months after the contract. Partner visa holders (subclass 309 or 820) can use the same tests.
- NSW **surcharge land tax** (5% of the land value each year) uses a different count. A permanent resident needs to be in Australia for 200 days or more in the calendar year. Spend 165 days or more overseas in a year and the surcharge can apply. Revenue NSW's land tax page only mentions permanent residents here, so if you're on a partner visa, check with Revenue NSW rather than assume the same rule applies.
- In **Victoria** (example), permanent residents are specifically excluded from the Foreign Purchaser Additional Duty (FPAD).

For every state and territory, see the [state-by-state table](/articles/buying-property-without-australian-pr-or-citizenship/#state-by-state) in our main visa article.

## Timing Matters

If your residency status changes before settlement, you may be able to get the surcharge back. In NSW, if you paid surcharge purchaser duty at exchange but you're no longer a foreign person on the date the property transfers to you (for example, PR came through and you meet the 200-day test), you can apply to Revenue NSW for a refund. Refunds aren't automatic, and you have 5 years from the original assessment to apply. Other states have their own processes, and some, such as Queensland, fix your status on the contract date, so getting PR before settlement doesn't help there.

## Key Takeaways

- Temporary visa = foreign person rules, FIRB approval, the established-home ban and surcharges apply.
- Permanent residency = no FIRB approval needed. Whether state surcharges stop depends on the state. In NSW, for example, you also need to meet the 200-day test.
- Check the timing of your PR grant, and your days in Australia, relative to exchange and settlement. This can save tens of thousands in surcharges.

Confirm your status with the ATO and your state revenue office before you sign a contract. Tax, duty and FIRB questions can get technical, so you may also need a lawyer or accountant.
